Congratulations! You made Community Day 2026 a huge success 🙌 Not only did the number of events around the world grow, almost every region saw average guest numbers almost double! Seeing what hosts prepared for their meetups was awe-inspiring, and hearing the stories from around the world after the event was incredibly heartwarming. Thank you to every host and guest who participated in this year’s Community Day, you knocked it out of the park! Let’s dig into the largest community gathering in the smart home industry…

Before I get into the meat of my post, let me just say, from everyone in the Community department at the Open Home Foundation…

**THANK YOU!** From **host** to **guest**, each and every one of you made this year’s Community Day amazing 🥰

**To the hosts**: Thank you so much for your willingness to shepherd all those who may have felt they didn’t have a local group to bond with. You’ve proven to them that there _are_ like-minded people in your community who are excited to come together and talk about their smart homes 💪

**To the guests**: We are sincerely humbled by your attendance. I may not speak for every host around the world, but I can say that both Franck and I are grateful to those who attended our meetup in Enschede, the Netherlands, and I’m sure other hosts likely feel similar 😌

**To our <a href="https://works-with.home-assistant.io/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Works with Home Assistant</a> partners**: Our deepest gratitude for you showing up this year to provide sample devices to hosts, let’s work closer together next year to smooth this process out 🤝

Now, let’s chat about some numbers 🤓

## Stats for the data nerds

If you’re like me – _and a lot of you are_ – you really enjoy the numbers that come with hosting events like this. And boy are there lots of good numbers to share.

<a href="https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2025/06/24/community-day-2025-wrap-up/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Last year</a> we had 26 hours of continuous partying, this year we bumped that up to 34.5. Starting at 14:30 UTC on Friday, November 6, in Budapest, Hungary, and ending at 01:00 UTC on Sunday, November 8, in Seattle, Washington, USA – city after city kicked off their meetup with their local community.

I’ll admit, I don’t think any of us expected y’all to host your meetups outside of the official date, making it almost a whole weekend affair, but we love to see it!

There were a total of 97 events in 35 countries: up 20% on last year and just shy of our goal of 100. There was also a 76% increase in registered guests to 2,712, upping the average up to 28 people per meetup! Australia really showed up this year, which helped grow the Australia/New Zealand region by eight-fold, and we also saw some excellent growth in the Africa and Asia/Pacific regions.

While after the 2025 event we mentioned lowering the capacity limit for meetups to less than 20 people – an estimate we originally based on meetup attendance from our own events – the massive turnout this year has us heading back to the drawing board for our guidelines around meetup sizes 😅

My favorite part of coordinating this event with the community is seeing how the hosts try to make their guests feel welcome and special. While we did provide merchandise from our official store to a good portion of the events (66 requested some this year), several hosts ordered or created locally produced goodies to share with their guests.

## A worldwide event is tough to manage

Of course, running an event at this scale doesn’t come without challenges, and even though this year was a success, there were new issues we had to mitigate the best we could. What we learned the most is which processes we can improve for next year’s event. Nothing here is a promise of what’s to come, but notes of things that we’re going to discuss as a team and foundation.

🛍️ Improving the merch request process: My dream for next year is for the foundation to have a hidden store or coupons for hosts, so they can request merch directly from the store, instead of the complicated internal process we managed.

👕 Custom swag for the event: In addition to using our official merchandise, our ideal scenario is to also source artists around the world to help us create custom designs specific to their regions.

✉️ Implementing a ticketing system to handle communications: This will make sure everyone can see who responded to what, and hosts don’t have to think about who they are replying to.

🗓️ More of a heads up on the event’s next date: Even with a three-month lead-up time from when we announced the save-the-date, we believe we can do better here (more on that at the end of this post).

🤝 Engaging our Works with Home Assistant partners earlier to provide cool devices to hosts: Next year we plan on having partners reach out directly to hosts through Luma, rather than first relying on us to ask the hosts’ permission to share their details.

📄 Updated Community Meetups Playbook: We’ve spotted a ton of gaps we had to cover in email communications that should be addressed directly in the playbook, so we’re going to do a nice overhaul of it to account for these.

One thing we do at the foundation with every event we host or manage is a retrospective meeting to discuss both the good things and the items-to-be-improved, so I expect we’ll add more to the list of things we’re going to improve for next year’s event.

## Early feedback from hosts

Through Luma, we sent hosts a feedback form about how this year’s event was handled from their perspective. We’ll comb through all of it before we get together for the retrospective, but here’s what some had to say so far 🧐

- The Saturday date works well for most, but having at least 12 weeks of lead-up time would be good. This is because some venues, like libraries or universities for instance, require 60 to 90 days notice to reserve spaces.
- Having more guides and tutorials on hand for hosts would be preferred.
- Communication from the core team was good, but could be improved.
- Offering editable assets for events will help with marketing the meetups locally.
- A centralized portal for hosts, where they can request merch and submit reimbursements, would provide a better experience to manage these events.

Even though this is just a portion of what hosts have told us, we already have some really valuable insights. If you hosted a Community Day meetup, double check your email (and spam/junk folders) for a message from the Open Home Foundation Luma calendar with a link to the feedback form so we can be sure to include your responses during our retrospective!
