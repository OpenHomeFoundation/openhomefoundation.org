Privacy is one of the Open Home Foundation's core principles, so it was a
natural place for our first formal study. We asked two questions: what do
people do to keep their smart homes private, and which parts of smart home
technology make them uncomfortable enough to change course?

Before each interview, participants wrote a list of best practices for a friend
setting up a private smart home. We also shared the task with the community on
the Home Assistant forum. The interviews then explored how participants rated
the privacy of their own setup, who they were protecting it from, and how their
advice compared with what they actually do.

Privacy concerns in the smart home don't exist in a vacuum. People are
responding to a broader landscape of data breaches, surveillance capitalism, AI
trained on personal data, and tracking. We presented the work as a poster at ACM
CHI 2026, the leading international conference on human–computer interaction
(the study of how people use and experience technology).
